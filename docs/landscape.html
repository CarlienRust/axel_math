South Africa math app landscape: existing solutions and market gaps

Existing solutions

MyMathsApp
CAPS Free SA-built
Grades 1–9, practice questions. Requires internet. No gamification. Backed by WorksheetCloud (subscription).
iRainbow
CAPS Paid SA-built Gamified
Gr 1–12, AI-powered. English & Afrikaans. Requires device purchase or subscription. Limited free access.
Siyavula
CAPS Zero-rated SA-built
Gr 7–12, adaptive practice. Zero-rated on MTN & Vodacom. Maths & Science only. No foundation phase coverage.
MathsUp
CAPS SA-built Grade R only
Targets Grade R teachers & parents. isiXhosa, isiZulu, English, Afrikaans. Android & mobi. Very narrow scope.
WorksheetCloud
CAPS Paid SA-built
Gr 1–9, exam prep focus. Strong reporting. Requires subscription ~R200/month. Not free-tier accessible.
Khan Academy
Free Some offline
Global, not SA-specific. Not CAPS-aligned. Data-heavy. English only. No local context or characters.
Mathletics
Paid Gamified
Australian curriculum base. Has SA presence but not natively CAPS-aligned. Subscription required.
IXL Maths
CAPS-mapped Paid
SA curriculum mapped. Good practice. US product, not SA context. Subscription. No offline mode.

Where the gap is — your opportunity

Low-end device + offline-first: No existing SA math app works fully offline on a basic Android phone. Most need reliable data. A lightweight Progressive Web App (PWA) or small APK changes this fundamentally.
Foundation phase gap (Gr R–3, ages 5–9): MathsUp covers only Grade R. Nobody owns the critical concrete-to-abstract bridge for the youngest learners, which is where SA's innumeracy crisis starts.
Indigenous language support: Most apps offer English & Afrikaans only. A truly SA app could include isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sepedi — reaching millions of children who think in those languages first.
Genuinely free at point of use: Every SA-built option either has a paywall or requires a school license. A zero-cost model (ad-free, donor/NGO-funded, or zero-rated) removes the single biggest access barrier.
Local cultural context: Khan Academy uses US examples. An SA app can use taxi ranks, local markets, and familiar SA characters — making maths feel relevant, not foreign.
